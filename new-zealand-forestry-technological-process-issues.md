| Source location | Government page/search-result text |
| Relevant population, place, or context | New Zealand harvesting, log handling, transport, and logistics |
| Tags | automation; log sorting; log landings; productivity; residue; labour |
| Duplicate check | New |

**Faithful evidence note**

MPI identifies a productivity constraint at the log landing, stating that while felling and extraction productivity increased through the earlier Steepland Harvesting Programme, the harvest process is bottlenecked by log-sorting productivity. It states that log-landing bottlenecks drive larger landings, more earthworks, and higher sediment and wood-residue runoff during heavy rain. The programme describes development work on automation for log sorting, tagging, residue chipping, and load securing.

**Collection note**

This record describes constraints and technology-development activities stated by MPI; it does not evaluate their effectiveness.

**Faithful evidence note**

The report states that some projects experienced delays and that the in-field debarker project was terminated. It attributes the termination to reduced volumes to China requiring debarking, lower-than-expected domestic demand, increased fixed debarking facilities, and the investment and time required to develop a commercial product. The report also states that a slow market affected some development projects and sales of commercialised products, and that programme expenditure was behind plan.

**Collection note**

The record reports statements from the programme update and does not assess whether the terminated project should have proceeded.

The report says the PlantMax machine did not provide adequate site preparation to meet the requirements of a Central North Island forest company, limiting its potential in that region.
