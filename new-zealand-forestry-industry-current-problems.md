**Faithful evidence note**

MPI states that forestry export revenue is expected to ease 1% to $6.1 billion in the year to 30 June 2026, primarily due to softer log prices associated with moderating demand in China. It forecasts a further 2% decline in 2026–27, linked on the page to ongoing cost pressures and reduced harvest and production volumes.

**Faithful evidence note**

MPI lists labour shortages, rising costs, social licence, legal changes, and constraints on harvesting-system productivity as inter-related sector challenges. The page states that increased harvest would require around 100 additional contracting crews and 700 additional workers in harvesting and log transport from current levels, described as a 20% increase. It also states that research indicates a $10-per-cubic-metre increase in harvesting costs could make 9% to 11% more of the small forest resource uneconomic to harvest.

The source is an MPI programme page. The retrieved material did not provide a single publication date for the challenge statements.

MPI states that changes to the National Environmental Standards for Commercial Forestry came into force on 4 June 2026. The guidance says forestry slash may be moved off a harvest site by small landslides and that, on higher-risk land, a Slash Mobilisation Risk Assessment is required from 4 June 2026 to identify whether a resource consent is needed.
